# Movement direction and upstream tracking

The [upstream track-point definition](https://github.com/prcvlabs/multiview/blob/7e334db96ca7ab685628365a2eaa2d5e8117eac3/multiview/multiview_cpp/src/perceive/cost-functions/tracklets/track-point.hpp) stores position, frame and a gaze-direction angle in radians. Its gaze is an orientation estimate, not automatically the direction a person is moving. The [upstream repository](https://github.com/prcvlabs/multiview) provides a multi-camera tracking pipeline under Apache-2.0, but its documented build targets older CUDA and system dependencies; full integration has not yet been built or evaluated in this WSL environment. Its data and model dependencies have their own terms. Existing data include published floor tracks; they do not prove our model hypotheses are correct.

The site03 floor view now optionally displays direction arrows for the current analysis source. Each arrow is a research-only displacement vector from six consecutive valid samples, spanning five frame intervals. A net displacement smaller than 0.1 scene units, a missing sample, spatial conflict, or insufficient history produces no arrow. The rule never interpolates over gaps or infers facing or gaze. The threshold and window are exploratory and not physically calibrated; noise, tracking switches and localization error may still produce wrong directions. The analysis time window applies before direction computation.

The JSON research export includes the active frame, method, threshold, intervals, visibility setting and computed current-frame estimates alongside source hashes. Unit tests cover straight movement, short history, a missing frame and no displacement; browser inspection at frame 100 showed two arrows and the toggle hid them. A missing arrow means direction unknown or below the threshold, not necessarily that nobody moved.
